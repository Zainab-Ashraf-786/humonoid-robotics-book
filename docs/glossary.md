# Course Glossary

This glossary defines key terms used throughout the Physical AI & Humanoid Robotics course.

## A

**AI (Artificial Intelligence)** - The simulation of human intelligence processes by machines, especially computer systems.

## B

**Behavior Trees** - A mathematical model of action sequences used in robotics and AI to represent the behavior of a robot or agent.

## C

**Collision Detection** - The computational problem of detecting the intersection of two or more geometric objects in a 3D environment.

## D

**Digital Twin** - A virtual representation of a physical object or system that simulates its real-world behavior.

**Docker** - A set of platform as a service products that use OS-level virtualization to deliver software in packages called containers.

## E

**Embodied AI** - AI systems that are integrated with physical bodies and interact with the real world.

**Embodied Intelligence** - Intelligence that emerges from the interaction between an agent and its environment.

## G

**Gazebo** - A 3D simulation environment for robotics that provides realistic physics simulation and rendering.

## H

**Humanoid Robot** - A robot that has a physical structure similar to that of a human body.

## I

**Isaac Sim** - NVIDIA's robotics simulator for developing and testing AI-based robotics applications.

**Isaac ROS** - A collection of hardware-accelerated perception and navigation packages for ROS.

## L

**LiDAR** - Light Detection and Ranging, a remote sensing method that uses light in the form of a pulsed laser to measure distances.

## M

**Module** - A self-contained unit of the course that focuses on a specific aspect of Physical AI and robotics.

## N

**Navigation** - The process of planning and following paths for a robot to move from one location to another.

**Nav2** - The ROS 2 navigation stack that provides capabilities for autonomous navigation.

## P

**Perception** - The ability of a robot to understand its environment through sensor data.

**Physical AI** - A field that combines artificial intelligence with physical systems to create embodied, intelligent agents.

**Python** - A high-level programming language commonly used in robotics and AI development.

## R

**ROS (Robot Operating System)** - A flexible framework for writing robot software that provides a collection of tools, libraries, and conventions.

**ROS 2** - The second generation of the Robot Operating System with improved security, real-time support, and multi-robot support.

**ROS 2 Nodes** - Processes that perform computation in the ROS 2 system.

**ROS 2 Topics** - ROS 2 messages published by nodes to communicate with one another.

**ROS 2 Services** - Request/response communication pattern in ROS 2.

**ROS 2 Actions** - Goal-based communication pattern in ROS 2.

## S

**Sensor Simulation** - The process of modeling how physical sensors would behave in a virtual environment.

**Simulation** - The imitation of the operation of a real-world process or system over time.

## T

**Teleoperation** - The remote control of a robot by a human operator.

**Transform** - The representation of position and orientation in space, commonly used in robotics.

## U

**Unity** - A cross-platform game engine that can be used for creating 3D robotics simulations.

**URDF (Unified Robot Description Format)** - An XML format for representing a robot model in ROS.

## V

**VSLAM (Visual Simultaneous Localization and Mapping)** - The process of calculating the position and orientation of a robot by using input from visual sensors.

**VLA (Vision-Language-Action)** - Framework combining visual, linguistic, and motor capabilities in robotics.

## I

**IMU (Inertial Measurement Unit)** - An electronic device that measures and reports a body's specific force, angular rate, and sometimes the magnetic field surrounding the body.
